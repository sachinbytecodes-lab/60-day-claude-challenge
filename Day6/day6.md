### ⚠️ Issues Identified in Original

1. **No Professional Summary** — ATS and recruiters expect an introductory keyword-rich paragraph.
2. **Icons used in contact line** — Characters like `Ó`, `R`, `¯`, `½*` render as garbage in ATS parsers, corrupting name/contact extraction.
3. **Section heading "PROFILE"** — Non-standard. ATS parsers look for "Summary" or "Professional Summary".
4. **Skills section incomplete** — OpenCV, MediaPipe, Chart.js, NLP, Computer Vision appeared in project descriptions but were absent from Skills.
5. **Mixed verb tenses** — "Architect a fault-tolerant…" (present) mixed with past-tense bullets.
6. **Tech stack buried in descriptions** — Keywords like Firebase, MediaPipe, Chart.js need their own labeled line to be parsed.
7. **Inconsistent date formatting** — "Sep. 2024", "Jun 2026", "2020 – 2024" used different conventions.
8. **Keyword inconsistency** — "Media-Pipe" vs "MediaPipe" used interchangeably; ATS treats these as different tokens.

## 🔄 What Changed & Why

### 1. ✅ Added Professional Summary
**Why it matters:** ATS systems assign heavy weight to the top section. Without a summary, keyword density is low and the recruiter has no immediate context.  
**Change:** Wrote a 3-sentence summary targeting AI/Data roles, incorporating terms like "computer vision," "data analytics," "AI engineering," and "voice assistants."

---

### 2. ✅ Removed Icons & Special Characters
**Why it matters:** Characters like `Ó`, `½*`, `¯` are decorative symbols that ATS parsers read as garbage — they can corrupt name parsing and contact extraction entirely.  
**Change:** Replaced all icons with plain-text separators (`|`).

---

### 3. ✅ Renamed "PROFILE" → "PROFESSIONAL SUMMARY"
**Why it matters:** ATS systems parse section headings by matching known labels. "Profile" is non-standard and may be skipped entirely.  
**Change:** Renamed to "Professional Summary" — universally recognized.

---

### 4. ✅ Expanded Skills with Missing Keywords
**Why it matters:** OpenCV, MediaPipe, NLP, Computer Vision, and Chart.js appear in project descriptions but were absent from Skills. ATS often parses Skills separately — if a keyword isn't there, the system misses it.  
**Change:** Added all relevant tools/concepts found in project sections to the Skills block.

---

### 5. ✅ Standardized Action Verbs (Past Tense)
**Why it matters:** Inconsistent tenses signal poor writing and confuse parsers that extract achievements.  
**Change:** All project/experience bullets rewritten with consistent past-tense active verbs: Developed, Architected, Built, Implemented, Engineered, Analyzed, Applied.

---

### 6. ✅ Separated Tech Stack as Its Own Line
**Why it matters:** When tools are buried in sentence-form bullets, ATS keyword extractors often miss them or associate them with the wrong context.  
**Change:** Added a dedicated `Tech:` line under each project listing all tools used.

---

### 7. ✅ Standardized Date Formatting
**Why it matters:** Inconsistent formats (Sep. vs Sep, Jun vs June) can confuse date-parsing modules in ATS systems.  
**Change:** All dates use `Mon YYYY – Mon YYYY` format consistently.

---

### 8. ✅ Deduplicated Keyword "MediaPipe"
**Why it matters:** "Media-Pipe" and "MediaPipe" are treated as two different tokens by ATS engines — splitting keyword frequency.  
**Change:** Standardized to "MediaPipe" throughout the document.

## 🧠 Key Learnings

### On ATS Systems
- **ATS parsers are literal** — they match exact strings. "Media-Pipe" ≠ "MediaPipe". Standardize every tool name to its official spelling.
- **Section heading names matter** — use "Work Experience", "Professional Summary", "Certifications" not creative alternatives.
- **Icons break parsing** — decorative characters (bullets, arrows, special symbols) are read as raw Unicode and corrupt adjacent text in most ATS engines.
- **Skills section is parsed separately** — always mirror keywords from your project/experience bullets into the Skills section explicitly.

### On Resume Writing
- **A Professional Summary is not optional** — it's the first thing both ATS and humans read. It should frontload your strongest keywords and role target.
- **Verbs set the tone** — "Developed", "Engineered", "Architected" convey impact; "worked on", "helped with" do not.
- **One page discipline** — removing redundancy (repeated tool names, verbose descriptions) forces clarity and strengthens impact-per-line.
- **Truthfulness over polish** — never fabricate metrics or achievements. A clean, honest resume outperforms an embellished one that unravels in interviews.

### On Student Resumes Specifically
- **Projects are your experience** — treat them with the same rigor as work history. Separate tech stack, lead with impact, use strong verbs.
- **Certifications signal initiative** — list them with issuer + date for credibility and ATS keyword capture (e.g., "Oracle Cloud Infrastructure" is a searchable term).
- **Simulations count** — Forage/virtual job simulations are legitimate experience entries when framed correctly.

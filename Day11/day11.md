## 🧠 Key Learnings

### 1. ATS is about keyword alignment, not keyword stuffing
ATS systems scan for exact or near-exact matches to job description terms. Simply having the skill isn't enough — the *phrasing* must match. "RESTful API" scores higher than just "API work."

### 2. Never fabricate — only realign
The golden rule of ATS optimization: if it's not in your experience, don't add it. Instead, find the closest honest equivalent (e.g., Oracle Cloud cert instead of AWS) and surface it prominently.

### 3. Action verbs dramatically increase bullet strength
Weak: *"Developed a system..."*
Strong: *"Engineered a fault-tolerant, offline-first pipeline..."*
ATS and human recruiters both respond to specific, results-oriented language.

### 4. A Professional Summary is non-negotiable for ATS
The original resume had no summary section — a major miss. ATS algorithms weight the top of the document heavily. A keyword-rich, role-specific summary paragraph can alone raise your score by 8–12 points.

### 5. Skill section structure matters
Grouping skills into labeled rows (`Frameworks:`, `API Development:`, `Cloud / DevOps:`) helps ATS parsers correctly categorize your abilities, instead of treating them as one undifferentiated blob of text.

### 6. Honest gap acknowledgment builds credibility
Identifying skills you *don't* have (React, Docker, AWS) is just as important as highlighting what you do have. Recruiters notice inflated resumes — gaps acknowledged honestly are far better than fabricated skills discovered in interviews.

### 7. AI as a career tool, not a shortcut
Using Claude AI for this task wasn't about bypassing effort — it was about applying a systematic, structured process (gap analysis → keyword mapping → rewrite → validation) that would take hours manually, done in minutes while maintaining full accuracy.

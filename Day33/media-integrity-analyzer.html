Headline Detective

Headlines are written to make you click — that's their job. But sometimes they stretch facts, imply things the article never proves, or use emotionally loaded words to seem bigger than they are. Learning to spot the gap between a headline and the actual story is one of the most useful media literacy skills you can build, because headlines are often all people ever read.

Emotion Detector

Media doesn't just inform — it aims for a feeling: outrage, fear, envy, urgency, belonging. Once you feel something strongly, you're more likely to share, click, or believe without checking. Spotting the emotional lever being pulled on you is the first step to responding with your head, not just your gut.

    Three habits to take with you

    • 1
      Read past the headline.
      Skim the first two paragraphs before reacting or sharing — headlines are ads for the article, not the article.
    • 2
      Name the emotion.
      If something makes you feel outraged, thrilled, or scared fast, pause. Ask: "what does this post want me to do next?"
    • 3
      Check the source, not just the story.
      Who published this, and what do they gain if you believe it, click it, or share it?
